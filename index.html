Portugal's Innovation Slowdown

After a pandemic-era peak, the share of Portuguese companies innovating has fallen for two survey periods in a row. Here's what the numbers say — and don't say.

By Afonso Rodrigues

Every two years, the National Statistics Institute (INE) and the DGEEC ask thousands of Portuguese companies the same basic question: did you innovate? In the 2022–2024 round of the Community Innovation Survey, 42.5% of companies with 10 or more employees said yes — they had developed some kind of innovation activity, from a new product to an internal R&D project.

That sounds healthy, until you look at where that number came from.

1. A pandemic bump, now fading

Innovation activity in Portugal didn't rise steadily. It spiked to 48.0% in 2018–2020 and has been sliding ever since. The DGEEC's own explanation points to COVID-19: companies were forced to overhaul how they worked almost overnight, and much of that scramble counted as process innovation. As the emergency eased, so did the rate.

Share of companies with 10+ employees reporting innovation activity, by type and survey period. Source: INE / DGEEC, CIS 2022–2024.

Process innovation is the clearest example: it jumped nearly 15 points between 2016–18 and 2018–20, then gave almost half of that back over the next two survey rounds. Product innovation, by contrast, barely moved — it's the steadier of the two.

2. Size and sector decide who innovates

The 42.5% headline hides enormous gaps. Large companies — 250 employees or more — innovate at almost double the national rate. So do two sectors in particular: information & communication, and financial & insurance services.

Share of companies reporting innovation activity, by group, 2022–2024. Source: INE / DGEEC, CIS 2022–2024.

3. Almost everyone is cooling off — except industry

Compared with the previous survey round (2020–2022), innovation activity fell in nearly every sector. Transport & storage and financial services cooled the fastest. Industry was the one exception, posting the only increase of any sector.

Percentage-point change in share of innovating companies, 2020–22 to 2022–24. Source: INE / DGEEC, CIS 2022–2024.

4. Where the money actually goes

Despite fewer companies innovating, total spending on innovation activities rose sharply in 2024, reaching €4,865.1 million — up from €3,382.4 million in 2022. That money is heavily concentrated: Greater Lisbon and the North together account for 75% of it.

Innovation spending by region, 2024, € millions. Source: INE / DGEEC, CIS 2022–2024.

A slice of that spending has a green label attached: 25.4% of companies introduced an innovation with some environmental benefit, and the money behind it — €1,448.3 million — made up 29.8% of all innovation spending in 2024.

5. Guarding the idea: trademarks over patents

When Portuguese companies do protect their innovations, they overwhelmingly reach for a trademark rather than a patent. Registering a brand is cheap and fast; patenting an invention is neither, and it shows in the numbers.

Share of companies using each intellectual property instrument, 2022–2024. Source: INE / DGEEC, CIS 2022–2024.

The takeaway

Portugal's innovation story isn't one of steady progress. It's a pandemic-era spike that is still working its way back down, propped up by a small group of large, urban, tech- and finance-heavy companies — while spending climbs even as participation shrinks.
